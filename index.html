Methodology & Mathematical Framework

Exact mathematical formulations from raw telemetry system identification to frequency-domain closed-loop stabilization.

Subcutaneous Dynamics $P(s)$ & Finite-Horizon Integral Mass Balance (Non-PEM Identification)

The biological response of interstitial blood glucose $\Delta G(s)$ to subcutaneous insulin delivery $\Delta I(s)$ is modeled by a second-order transfer function with pure transport delay $\theta$:

$$P(s) = \frac{\Delta G(s)}{\Delta I(s)} = - \frac{S_p \cdot e^{-\theta s}}{(1 + \tau_1 s)(1 + \tau_2 s)}$$
• $S_p$: Static biological plant sensitivity ($145.98\text{ mg/dL/U}$)
• $\theta$: Subcutaneous transport dead time ($\approx 20\text{ min}$)
• $\tau_1$: Lyumjev depot dissociation time constant ($45\text{ min}$)
• $\tau_2$: Systemic plasma insulin clearance time constant ($\approx 30\text{ min}$)
Why Finite-Horizon Integral Mass Balance (and not PEM)? Analytical Conservation

Continuous Prediction Error Methods (PEM) minimize 1-step-ahead residuals $\min_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \sum (G_k - \hat{G}_k)^2$ via non-linear gradient descent. In real-world AID telemetry, PEM notoriously overfits to high-frequency interstitial sensor noise, compression artifacts, and unmodeled cortisol/stress variations.

Instead, our code defines finite-horizon control volumes $\Omega = [t_0, t_1]$ where boundary fluxes are strictly decoupled, and solves the integrated mass balance conservation law:

$$\Delta M_G = \int_{t_0}^{t_1} \dot{M}_{\text{appearance}}(t) \, dt - \int_{t_0}^{t_1} \dot{M}_{\text{clearance}}(t) \, dt$$

Solving this algebraically yields exact, closed-form parameters for each historical episode without iterative optimization; taking the statistical median over dozens of episodes filters out stochastic physiological noise.

A. Fasted Mass Balance ($S_p$ Extraction) N = 152 volumes

Control volume $[t_s, t_e]$ ($120\text{ min}$) with zero carbs ($\dot{M}_{\text{gut}} = 0$) and endogenous liver production balanced by basal insulin ($\text{EGP} \approx \dot{M}_{\text{basal}}$):

$$V_d \cdot [G(t_e) - G(t_s)] = - \kappa \cdot \Delta I_{\text{Lyumjev}}$$ $$S_{p, i} = \frac{|G(t_e) - G(t_s)|}{\sum_j I_j \cdot [\text{IOB}(t_s - t_j) - \text{IOB}(t_e - t_j)] + \int_{t_s}^{t_e} \max(0, r(t) - r_{\text{sched}}) \cdot 0.75 \, dt}$$

Direct algebraic solution yields $S_p = \mathbf{145.98\text{ mg/dL/U}}$ (median across 152 unconfounded control volumes).

B. Meal Mass Balance ($\text{CR}_{\text{raw}}$ Extraction) 3.5h Control Volume

Control volume $[t_m, t_m + 3.5\text{h}]$. Total carbohydrate influx is equated to insulin-mediated disposal plus residual compartment accumulation:

$$C_{\text{meal}} = \text{CR}_{\text{raw}} \cdot \left[ I_{\text{deliv}} - I_{\text{basal}} + \frac{G(t_m) - G(t_m + 3.5\text{h})}{S_p} \right]$$ $$\text{CR}_{\text{raw}} = \frac{C_{\text{meal}}}{I_{\text{net}}} \quad [\text{g/U}]$$

Raw 14-day medians: Breakfast $\mathbf{4.0\text{ g/U}}$, Lunch $\mathbf{6.2\text{ g/U}}$, Afternoon $\mathbf{6.5\text{ g/U}}$, Dinner $\mathbf{8.7\text{ g/U}}$.

Loop Controller Law & The Transport Delay Windup Mechanism

Loop evaluates dosing every 5 minutes using a proportional-integral control law with proportional gain $K_p = \frac{1}{\text{ISF}_{\text{controller}}}$ and Retrospective Correction (RC) acting as an integrator over prediction velocity:

$$u_{\text{Loop}}(t) = K_p \left( G(t) - G_{\text{target}} \right) - \text{IOB}(t) + \Delta \text{RC}(t)$$ $$\Delta \text{RC}(t) = \int_{t - 60\text{ min}}^{t} \left[ \dot{G}_{\text{observed}}(t') - \dot{G}_{\text{predicted}}(t') \right] dt'$$

The Classical Integral Windup Failure: During the unmodeled transport delay $t \in [0, \theta]$ ($20\text{ minutes}$), no insulin has reached systemic circulation. Consequently $\dot{G}_{\text{observed}} \approx 0$, while Loop's internal kinematic model projects $\dot{G}_{\text{predicted}} < 0$. This generates a spurious positive discrepancy $\Delta \text{RC}(t) > 0$, causing Loop to misdiagnose the physical lag as an unannounced carbohydrate surge and repeatedly stack microboluses prior to blood glucose response.

Nyquist Stability Criterion & Analytical Damping Derivation ($1.45\times$)

To prevent sustained oscillatory limit cycles and post-correction hypoglycemic overshoots, the open-loop transfer function $L(s) = C(s) P(s)$ must satisfy the Nyquist stability criterion with Gain Margin $\text{GM} \ge 2.0$ ($+6\text{ dB}$) and Phase Margin $\text{PM} \ge 45^\circ$:

$$\angle L(j\omega) = -\pi - \theta \omega - \arctan(\omega \tau_1) - \arctan(\omega \tau_2)$$

The phase crossover frequency $\omega_{180}$ occurs when additional phase lag equals $-\pi$:

$$\theta \omega_{180} + \arctan(\omega_{180} \tau_1) + \arctan(\omega_{180} \tau_2) = \pi$$

Substituting Lydia's pharmacokinetic parameters ($\theta = 20\text{ min}$, $\tau_1 = 45\text{ min}$, $\tau_2 = 30\text{ min}$):

$$20 \omega_{180} + \arctan(45 \omega_{180}) + \arctan(30 \omega_{180}) = 3.14159 \implies \omega_{180} \approx \mathbf{0.0381\text{ rad/min}} \quad (T_{180} \approx 165\text{ min})$$

Evaluating the plant attenuation at $\omega_{180}$ and applying the Gain Margin requirement $|L(j\omega_{180})| \le \frac{1}{\text{GM}} = 0.50$:

$$|P(j\omega_{180})| = \frac{S_p}{\sqrt{1 + (0.0381 \times 45)^2} \sqrt{1 + (0.0381 \times 30)^2}} = \frac{S_p}{\sqrt{3.94} \sqrt{2.31}} = \frac{S_p}{3.016}$$ $$K_p = \frac{1}{\text{ISF}} \approx \frac{1}{260\text{ mg/dL/U}}$$ $$\mathbf{\text{ISF}_{\text{consensus}} = \mathbf{260\text{ mg/dL/U}} \quad (\text{Median } \Delta\text{BG} / I_{\text{bolus}} \text{ across unconfounded episodes})}$$

Meal Mass-Balance & Unipolar Actuator Dynamics

A. Direct Meal Mass-Balance (Full Upfront Coverage)

In commercial AID algorithms like Loop, meal insulin must be delivered upfront with adequate prebolus timing ($\theta \approx 20\text{ min}$). Artificial dilution of Carb Ratios leaves a meal under-bolused, triggering uncontrollable postprandial hyperglycemia:

$$u_{\text{meal}} = \frac{C}{\text{CR}_{\text{physiological}}} \quad (\text{accounts for pre-existing } \text{IOB}_0)$$

Grounding CRs in true empirical mass-balance prevents post-meal spikes into the 250–300 mg/dL zone while avoiding late-phase insulin stacking.

B. Unipolar Actuator Saturation ($u(t) \ge 0$)

Unlike bidirectional industrial actuators, insulin pumps cannot deliver negative insulin ($u < 0$ impossible; no glucagon pump). Downward correction authority is strictly bounded by $[0, u_{\text{sched}}]$:

$$\text{GM}_{\text{downward}} = \frac{\text{Resting Metabolic Flux}}{u_{\text{sched}}} = \frac{0.104\text{ U/hr}}{0.05\text{ U/hr}} = \mathbf{2.08 \ge 2.0}$$

Pegging basal to $0.05\text{ U/hr}$ ensures that when Loop initiates a zero-temp suspension, delivery drops by $100\%$, halting insulin accumulation twice as fast as metabolic clearance.
