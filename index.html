First-Principles Mass Balance & Pharmacodynamic Methodology Stage 1 → Stage 2 → Stage 3

Continuous mass-balance equations solved across rolling 14-day telemetry in strict physical dependency order

1 Stage 1: Pharmacological ISF & Lyumjev Response Integral ($R_{\text{gut}} = 0, \; \text{BG}_{\text{bolus}} \ge 165\text{ mg/dL}$)

For any correction excursion without carbohydrate confounding ($R_{\text{gut}} = 0$), the insulin that drives the drop between peak (zenith) and trough (nadir) is the exact time-integral of Lyumjev activity:

$$I_{\text{acted}} = \int_{t_{\text{zenith}}}^{t_{\text{nadir}}} A_{\text{Lyumjev}}(t) \, dt \equiv \text{IOB}(t_{\text{zenith}}) - \text{IOB}(t_{\text{nadir}})$$

Because Lydia uses ultra-rapid Lyumjev ($t_{\text{peak}} \approx 45\text{ min}$, $\text{DIA} \approx 300\text{ min}$), typically $15\%\text{–}35\%$ of the bolus has not yet metabolized when the glucose nadir is reached at 2.5–3.5 hours. Evaluating instantaneous sensitivity yields:

$$\text{ISF}_{\text{instantaneous}} = \frac{\text{BG}_{\text{zenith}} - \text{BG}_{\text{nadir}}}{\text{IOB}(t_{\text{zenith}}) - \text{IOB}(t_{\text{nadir}})} \approx 280\text{–}320\text{ mg/dL/U}$$

However, automated insulin delivery (Loop) relies on total bolus drop prediction. Setting ISF above $280\text{ mg/dL/U}$ in the pump causes Loop to undersize micro-boluses and prematurely suspend basal. We therefore set profile controller ISF to the robust direct-drop median:

$$\mathbf{\text{ISF}_{\text{controller}} = \text{median}\left(\frac{|\text{BG}_{\text{nadir}} - \text{BG}_{\text{bolus}}|}{I_{\text{delivered}}}\right) = 260\text{ mg/dL/U}}$$

2 Stage 2: Multi-Block Basal Rates (Resting Equilibrium & Dawn Surge Intercept)

In resting equilibrium ($\text{Carbs} = 0, \; \frac{d\text{BG}}{dt} \approx 0$), endogenous hepatic glucose production matches insulin clearance. Across 49 resting blocks, true metabolic requirements are solved by:

$$\text{Basal}_{\text{eq}}(t) = \frac{I_{\text{boluses}} + \int r_{\text{pump}}(t) \, dt}{\Delta t} - \frac{\text{BG}(t_2) - \text{BG}(t_1)}{\text{ISF} \cdot (t_2 - t_1)}$$
A. Deep Overnight vs. Dawn Surge Inflection (`00:00`, `01:30`, `05:00`):

The mass balance reveals a marked circadian divergence: deep sleep (`00:00–05:00`) requires $0.12\text{–}0.14\text{ U/hr}$, while waking cortisol output (`05:00–08:30`) surges to $0.20\text{–}0.27\text{ U/hr}$. Setting a dedicated Dawn Intercept at `05:00` ($0.20\text{ U/hr}$) halts morning spikes at the source and stops Loop from cannibalizing correction boluses.

B. Daytime Meal Deconvolution (`08:30`):

Because daytime fasting is rare in toddlers, awake baseline needs are solved via linear regression across daytime meals:

$$I_{\text{tot}} - \frac{\Delta\text{BG}}{\text{ISF}} = \frac{1}{\text{CR}} \cdot \text{Carbs} + \big(\mathbf{B_{\text{day}}} \times \Delta t\big)$$

The regression intercept isolates true daytime baseline requirements ($0.15\text{ U/hr}$), providing stable afternoon coverage without mid-afternoon dips.

Finite-Horizon Meal Mass-Balance Integration:

$$\mathbf{\text{CR} = \frac{\text{Carbohydrates (g)}}{I_{\text{bolus}} + \int_{t_0}^{t_f} \big(r_{\text{actual}}(t) - B_{\text{baseline}}\big) \, dt + \frac{\text{BG}(t_f) - \text{BG}(t_0)}{\text{ISF}}}}$$

Deducting active basal deviations and unresolved glucose deltas confirms Lydia's biological circadian curve: morning resistance at breakfast ($1:5.0\text{ g/U}$), midday sensitivity peak at lunch ($1:10.0\text{ g/U}$), dinner settling at $1:7.0\text{ g/U}$, and bedtime protection at $1:15.0\text{ g/U}$.
