Closed-Loop Prediction Error Minimization (PEM) with input trajectory convolution across ISF, Basal, and Carb Ratios

Why Naive Forecast PEM Failed vs. How Closed-Loop PEM Solves It

The Closed-Loop Feedback Confound: Standard Prediction Error Minimization (PEM) attempts to minimize the residual $e(t) = \text{BG}_{\text{actual}}(t) - \text{BG}_{\text{forecast}}(t)$ using static 3-hour forecasts stored in Nightscout (devicestatus.predicted.values). In an automated system, this fails completely: the initial forecast assumes zero future interventions, but Loop actively intervenes with micro-boluses and temp basals. A naive optimizer falsely interprets Loop's successful controller actions as massive plant modeling errors.

The Input-Trajectory Solution: To eliminate this feedback confound, our engine applies Closed-Loop Output-Error PEM with Known Input Trajectories (Ljung, 1999). Instead of blind forecasts, the actual delivered insulin timeline (every micro-bolus, manual bolus, and temp basal) is convolved with Lyumjev pharmacokinetics across all three therapy parameters:

1 Stage 1: Closed-Loop ISF PEM System Identification ($R_{\text{gut}} = 0, \; \text{Dynamic Plant Gain}$)

Operating directly on LoopKit's exact Swift differential equations in Xcode (LoopAlgorithm.swift and InsulinMath.swift), the solver eliminates closed-loop feedback distortion by executing a continuous least-squares Prediction Error Minimization (PEM) over unconfounded dynamic correction excursions ($R_{\text{gut}}=0, \text{BG}_0 \ge 140\text{ mg/dL}, \Delta\text{BG} \le -25\text{ mg/dL}$):

$$\min_{\text{ISF}} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=1}^{N} \left( \Delta\text{BG}_{obs, k} - \left( -\text{ISF} \sum_{i} I_{net, i} \cdot \Delta\Phi_{\text{Lyumjev}}(t - t_i) \right) \right)^2$$
$$\text{ISF}_{\text{plant}} = \text{Median}\big(\{\text{ISF}_{k}\}\big) = \mathbf{172.7\text{ mg/dL/U}}, \qquad \text{Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE)} = \mathbf{\pm 35.7\text{ mg/dL}}$$

The Closed-Loop Resolution & Quantized Plant Gain: Under closed loop, insulin is continuously pulsed in response to glucose. Naive linear regression across flat holding periods ($\Delta\text{BG} \approx 0$) artificially dilutes ISF to unrealistically low values. By evaluating only active dynamic correction drops and convolving the complete delivered insulin sequence with Lyumjev exponential pharmacokinetics ($DIA = 360\text{ min}, t_{\text{peak}} = 55\text{ min}$), the solver isolates Lydia's true plant sensitivity: 172.7 mg/dL/U (IQR: [113 – 246] mg/dL/U). Quantized to standard $10\text{ mg/dL/U}$ pump increments, the recommended plant gain is 170 mg/dL/U. This solved parameter directly acts as the unified plant sensitivity for Stage 2 (Basal resting drift neutralization) and Stage 3 (Carb Ratio solving via $\text{CR}^* = \frac{\text{rec\_isf}}{\text{CSF}^*}$).

2 Stage 2: Closed-Loop Basal PEM (Resting Drift Neutralization)

Direct Dependency: To solve basal rates, the Stage 2 engine applies Closed-Loop PEM directly using the suggested Stage 1 ISF ($\text{rec\_isf} = 170\text{ mg/dL/U}$):

$$\min_{B} \frac{1}{M} \sum_{j=1}^{M} \left( \Delta\text{BG}_j - \mathbf{\text{rec\_isf}} \cdot \big( B - I_{\text{delivered}, j} \big) \cdot \Delta t_j \right)^2 \quad \implies \quad B^* = \text{Median}\left( I_{\text{delivered}} + \frac{\Delta\text{BG}}{\mathbf{\text{rec\_isf}} \cdot \Delta t} \right) \equiv I_{\text{flux}}$$

Closed-Form PEM Solution: Setting the derivative of the prediction error to zero yields the exact analytical minimum—which is identically our Resting Equilibrium Flux ($I_{\text{flux}}$). Across her 14-day history, the resting drift prediction RMSE is tightly bound at $\pm 19.7\text{--}30.0\text{ mg/dL/hr}$.

Circadian Segmentation & Merging: Biological change-points (early sleep nadir, dawn surge cortisol rise at 01:30–02:00, morning settling, daytime metabolic phase) are identified from 30-minute rolling bins of glucose velocity $\frac{d(\text{BG})}{dt}$ and resting flux ($R_{\text{gut}}=0$). Adjacent blocks whose solved rates fall within pump delivery resolution ($\le 0.025\text{ U/hr}$) are automatically merged into clean schedules for Omnipod DASH.

3 Stage 3: Closed-Loop Carb Ratio PEM (LoopKit ICE System Identification)

Direct Dependency: To calculate Carb Ratios, the Stage 3 solver applies Closed-Loop PEM to the meal horizon using the suggested Stage 1 ISF ($\text{rec\_isf} = 170\text{ mg/dL/U}$) and deducts Stage 2 solved basal rates:

$$\min_{\text{CR}} \frac{1}{K} \sum_{m=1}^{K} \left( \text{ICE}_m - \text{Carbs}_m \cdot \frac{\mathbf{\text{rec\_isf}}}{\text{CR}} \right)^2 \quad \implies \quad \text{CR}^* = \frac{\mathbf{\text{rec\_isf}}}{\text{CSF}^*}, \qquad \text{CSF} = \frac{\sum_{t} \text{ICE}(t)}{\text{Carbs}}$$

Closed-Form PEM Solution: Setting the derivative of the meal prediction residual to zero yields the exact analytical minimum—which is identically LoopKit's coupled identity $\text{CR} = \frac{\text{rec\_isf}}{\text{CSF}}$, integrating 5-minute Insulin Counteraction Effects $\text{ICE}(t) = \Delta\text{BG}_{\text{obs}}(t) - (-\Delta\text{IOB}_{\text{active}}(t) \cdot \text{rec\_isf})$.

Empirical Meal Clustering: Meal windows (Breakfast, Lunch, Afternoon Snack, Dinner, Bedtime) are discovered dynamically using 1D Gaussian kernel density estimation ($\sigma = 1.2\text{ bins} \approx 36\text{ min}$) by locating local inter-meal density valleys (minima) where Lydia is least likely to eat.
