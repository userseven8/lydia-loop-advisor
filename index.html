How to read this

Every setting below is estimated from a small number of events in a rolling 30-day window, so each one carries a 90% interval. A change is only suggested when that interval excludes the value already in the pump. “No change indicated” is the normal outcome — it means the data does not distinguish a better value, not that the current one has been confirmed.

“Discuss” means the evidence separates from the current setting enough to be worth raising with the diabetes care team. It is not a dose instruction, and nothing on this page should be entered into the pump without them.

Rows can read “no change” for different reasons: too few meals, a fit that explains little, meals still unsettled three hours later, or simply an interval that spans the current setting. The reason is stated in each row’s evidence column.

Basal blocks are set by a percentile of their day-to-day spread, not an average, because the rate runs unattended on every day including the low-demand ones. Overnight blocks use the 25th percentile; the daytime block uses the median. Her overnight requirement varies roughly tenfold night to night, which is what the loop exists to absorb and no fixed schedule can.

How each number is produced, and what it cannot show ISF from corrections → basal from fasting → ratios from meals

Every figure here is a statistical estimate from a small number of events, not a derivation. The stages run in sequence, so ISF error propagates into basal and then into the carb ratios, and the intervals shown do not capture that. Basal reads a 90-day history; the glucose metrics above stay on the rolling 30 days.

1 Stage 1: ISF from isolated corrections

To measure Lydia's true biological response to Lyumjev, we isolate nighttime correction events occurring with zero active carbohydrates ($R_{\text{gut}} = 0$) and no active overrides (illness, exercise, failing pod). We measure the drop from pre-bolus glucose to post-correction nadir:

$$\mathbf{\text{ISF}_{\text{physio}} = \text{median}\left(\frac{|\text{BG}_{\text{nadir}} - \text{BG}_{\text{bolus}}|}{I_{\text{delivered}}}\right) = 230\text{ mg/dL/U}}$$

Physiological Equilibrium & Opposing Error Cancellation:

During an isolated correction window, the complete blood glucose balance equation is:

$$\Delta\text{BG} = - \text{ISF} \cdot I_{\text{corr}} - \underbrace{\text{ISF} \cdot I_{\text{basal}}}_{\text{Deflates BG}} + \underbrace{\text{EGP}_{\text{liver}}}_{\text{Inflates BG}}$$

During resting periods without meals, basal delivery roughly matches endogenous hepatic glucose production ($\text{EGP}_{\text{liver}} \approx \text{ISF} \cdot I_{\text{basal}}$). The two unmodeled terms neutralize each other, leaving the marginal drop directly proportional to the correction bolus:

$$\Delta\text{BG} \approx - \text{ISF} \cdot I_{\text{corr}} \iff \text{ISF} \approx \frac{|\Delta\text{BG}|}{I_{\text{corr}}}$$

This biological equilibrium preserves stability: undercounting insulin and omitting liver production push in opposite directions and cancel, accurately isolating the net marginal sensitivity.

2 Stage 2: Basal — from dose–response, not from what is already set

Over a window with no food and no recent insulin, the rate that would have held glucose level is what was delivered, adjusted for how far glucose actually moved:

$$B_{\text{basal}} = \frac{\int r_{\text{pump}}(t) \, dt}{\Delta t} + \frac{\Delta\text{BG}}{\text{ISF}_{\text{physio}} \cdot \Delta t}$$

This estimator was circular and had to be repaired. It previously accepted only windows where glucose stayed between 70 and 180 at both ends — which keeps the moments the programmed rate happened to be working and discards every excursion that would have shown it wrong. Flat glucose at 0.40 U/hr “proved” 0.40; flat glucose at 0.05 “proved” 0.05. It was reporting the setting back, not measuring the requirement. It now accepts any physiologically plausible reading.

Three further conditions were needed before a window counts. No recent insulin, not just no food: with a 6 hour duration of action a bolus 2.5 h earlier is still working, and leaving it in made the daytime block read −17 mg/hr when the same windows read +5 without it. No hypo in the preceding hour, because “no recent insulin” otherwise selects the moments the loop withheld insulin because she was low, and the rebound that follows is not a basal signal. And one value per day rather than per window, since windows are one hour sliding every thirty minutes and pooling them lets a day contributing five windows outweigh a day contributing one.

Each block is then set at a percentile of its per-day spread rather than at the mean, because a basal rate runs unattended on every day in the block and has to be safe on the low-demand day, not the average one. Sleep blocks use the 25th percentile — she is unobserved and her overnight hypo exposure is already outside consensus. The daytime block uses the median, where she is supervised and inside target. Each row shows its own spread so you can see how wide it is.

The estimate above still partly echoed the setting. Regressing it against the rate programmed at the time left a residual slope of +0.26, because delivery during a window includes the scheduled basal itself. So the primary estimator is now a dose–response: her schedule has varied widely over the last 90 days, which is a natural experiment, and the rate at which fasting glucose drift crosses zero is the requirement. Drift involves no insulin arithmetic at all, so it cannot read the current setting back.

Two guards on it. Unobserved hours round down to the pump’s 0.05 grid, so quantising can never land above the estimate. And a crossing is only acted on when its interval is tight — the deep-night block returns a 4x span, wide enough that its point estimate carries little information, so that block holds regardless of where the centre falls. Where the schedule never varied enough to fit a slope, no crossing is reported and the block falls back to the per-day percentile.

What this still cannot do: it cannot see a period she never fasts through. Daytime windows are scarce because she eats hourly, so the daytime estimate draws on older data and each row states its age.

3 Stage 3: Carb ratios from isolated meals

Using the independently measured resting basal baseline, each 3-hour post-meal window determines the net target meal insulin required to return glucose flat to baseline ($\Delta\text{BG} = 0$):

$$I_{\text{meal}}^{\text{target}} = I_{\text{delivered}} - (B_{\text{resting}} \times 3\text{h}) + \frac{\Delta\text{BG}}{\text{ISF}_{\text{physio}}}$$

To prevent severe distortion from acute glucotoxicity spikes ($+112, +165\text{ mg/dL}$) or over-bolused rebound crashes ($-90\text{ mg/dL}$), Carb Ratios are fitted via Settled Target Linear Regression ($|\Delta\text{BG}| \le 60\text{ mg/dL}$), finding the true upfront ratio needed for flat postprandial return ($\text{CR} = \sum C_i^2 / \sum C_i I_{\text{meal}, i}^{\text{target}}$):

  • Breakfast & Morning (08:30–12:00): Morning cortisol increases insulin resistance; appropriate pre-bolus timing is critical.
  • Daytime Active (Lunch & Snack, 12:00–17:00): Active toddler daytime metabolism and physical activity.
  • Evening & Dinner (17:00–22:00): Evening meal digestion and bedtime settling.
  • Overnight Sleep Baseline (22:00–08:30): Overnight insulin sensitivity baseline protecting against nocturnal hypoglycemia.
